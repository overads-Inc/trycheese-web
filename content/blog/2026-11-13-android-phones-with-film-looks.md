There are more **Android phones with film looks** built into the camera than most people realise. Google, Samsung, Realme and Xiaomi all now ship some version of the idea, from Pixel's Camera Looks to Leica styles and Ricoh GR film tones.

They're not all the same thing, though. Some change how the phone processes the photo, some copy a colour, and some are closer to a classic preset. Here's what each one does, and how they compare with a film camera app.

## The quick comparison

| Phone | What it's called | What you get | Where to find it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pixel 11 family | Camera Looks | Natural, Shadows and Vanilla defaults, plus six stylised looks | Camera, Controls, Looks |
| Samsung Galaxy | My Filters | A filter built from the colours and style of any photo | Camera, Effects, Filters |
| Realme GT 8 Pro | Ricoh GR Film Tone | Standard, Positive Film, Negative Film, Monotone, Hi-Contrast B&W | The camera's GR features |
| Xiaomi Leica phones | Leica styles and filters | Leica Authentic, Leica Vibrant, plus Leica filters | Camera, Photo, top-right icon (Xiaomi 14T) |
| Any Android | Snapseed Camera | 11 film styles, free | The Snapseed app |

## Google Pixel 11: Camera Looks

The Pixel 11, 11 Pro, 11 Pro XL and 11 Pro Fold let you replace the Original Pixel look with one of three defaults: Natural, Shadows or Vanilla. There are six more stylised looks too: Digi, Black Tie, Minimal, Editorial, Classic and Velvet.

What makes Pixel's version interesting is how it's built. Google says it made adjustments such as merging fewer frames for a less processed feel, and rewriting colour lookup tables to emulate classic film chemistry. In other words, it's partly a change to the processing itself, not just a tint on top. Looks can be fine-tuned with sliders like Contrast and Highlights, and they don't work in Video mode. We cover the settings in [why phone photos look overprocessed](/blog/why-phone-photos-look-overprocessed).

## Samsung Galaxy: My Filters

Galaxy phones take a different approach. My Filters builds a reusable camera filter from any photo you choose, by mimicking its colours and style. You can save up to 99, and the feature's availability depends on your model and software. Build one from a scan of your own film photo and you've got a custom look. Our step-by-step guide to [a film look on a Samsung Galaxy](/blog/film-look-samsung-galaxy) shows how.

## Realme GT 8 Pro: Ricoh GR Film Tone

Realme worked with Ricoh, maker of the GR compact cameras, on the GT 8 Pro. Its product page lists five "RICOH GR Film Tone" options: Standard, Positive Film, Negative Film, Monotone and Hi-Contrast B&W. There are also GR recipes, with Green and Blue tones as examples.

The phone borrows other GR habits, like the classic 28mm and 40mm framing for street photography. Realme also pitches its processing as low on sharpening, smearing and AI generation. That's Realme's claim; we haven't tested it.

## Xiaomi: Leica styles and filters

Xiaomi's Leica-branded phones offer two photographic styles. On the Xiaomi 14T, you switch between them in the Camera app's Photo mode, using the icon at the top right. Xiaomi describes them like this:

- **Leica Authentic** aims for natural, true-to-life colour that preserves the original tones of the scene.
- **Leica Vibrant** boosts saturation and contrast for bold, striking images.

Leica's own description of Authentic goes further: moderate sharpening, realistic contrast, and reduced vignetting correction, so the corners fall off like a picture from a Leica Summicron lens. Leica's 2023 launch of the Xiaomi 13T also listed six Leica filters, including Leica Sepia and Leica Blue, adapted from a Leica M camera's Film Mode. Features vary by model, so check your phone's camera.

## Getting more from a built-in look

A few habits make any of these work better:

1. **Make it stick.** On a Pixel 11, Google's help page shows how to keep a stylised look on: Photo settings, More, then Always use selected Look. Otherwise it's easy to slip back to the default.
2. **Compare before you commit.** Samsung lets you swipe back to the Original filter in the viewfinder. Shoot the same scene both ways for a week and see which you keep.
3. **Test it at night.** Bright lights against darkness show quickly whether a look handles highlights well or just tints everything.

## Built-in look or film camera app?

A built-in look has one big advantage: it's right there in the camera you already use. Pixel's Camera Looks even change how many frames get merged. But most built-in looks are about colour and tone. None of the maker pages we read promise halation, and grain is rarely mentioned.

That's where apps come in. Snapseed's free camera includes 11 film styles and its editor adds Grain, Halation and Bloom tools; here's our guide to [Snapseed Camera on Android](/blog/snapseed-camera-android). Dedicated film camera apps go further still, as our [Android film camera roundup](/blog/film-camera-apps-for-android-2026) explains.

Whichever you pick, judge it the same way. Look for highlights that roll off softly, colour that responds rather than washes, grain that [changes with the tones](/blog/film-grain-vs-digital-noise), and glow [only around strong lights](/blog/what-is-halation). Our [six tells of a film look](/blog/what-makes-photos-look-like-film) go into more detail.
