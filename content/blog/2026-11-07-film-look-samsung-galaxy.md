You can get a convincing **film look on a Samsung Galaxy** with Samsung's own tools and one free app. It takes three layers: turn down the processing that makes phone photos look clinical, set the colour with Samsung's own My Filters, and add the texture film has, like grain and glow.

Galaxy phones have more of the tools than most people realise. Here's how to put them together, step by step.

## The quick version

1. **Install Camera Assistant** and turn off Auto HDR.
2. **Make a My Filter** from a film photo you love, ideally one of your own scans.
3. **Shoot with the filter**, then add grain in Snapseed, or shoot straight in Snapseed Camera.
4. **Judge it at night**, around bright lights, where fake film looks fall apart first.

## Step 1: Turn down the processing

A film look painted over a heavily processed photo rarely convinces, so start by easing off the processing. Samsung keeps its best controls in Camera Assistant, which you download from the Galaxy Store and then open from Camera settings.

Samsung's guide for the Galaxy S23 Ultra lists two settings worth changing:

- **Auto HDR: off.** HDR evens out bright and dark areas, which is exactly what film doesn't do.
- **Picture softening: on.** It smooths over-sharp edges and textures in Photo mode, which helps the crunchy look.

Options vary by model and One UI version. We explain why this matters, and the equivalents on iPhone and Pixel, in [why phone photos look overprocessed](/blog/why-phone-photos-look-overprocessed).

If you like to edit, **Expert RAW** is the other Samsung tool to know. It's a separate Galaxy Store app for the Galaxy S20 and later, and it saves a 16-bit Linear DNG RAW file alongside each JPEG, with manual ISO, shutter speed, white balance, exposure and focus. Samsung pitches it as a way to take HDR photos with more editing room, so it isn't unprocessed. It just gives you more to work with.

## Step 2: Copy a film colour with My Filters

This is the Galaxy's secret weapon. My Filters builds a reusable camera filter from any photo you choose, by mimicking its colours and style. Samsung's steps:

1. Open the Camera app in **PHOTO** mode.
2. Tap the **Effects** icon (two stacked squares) at the top, then **Filters**.
3. Tap **+**, then **Create filter**, and pick a photo from your Gallery.
4. Rename it if you like, then tap **Create**.

You can save up to 99 of them. Swipe to your filter in the viewfinder, and compare against **Original** before you shoot. You can also change a filter's intensity, or swap it, later in Gallery under Edit. Samsung notes My Filters isn't available at the 64MP 3:4 setting, and that availability depends on your model and software.

The trick is in the source photo. Use a frame you shot on real film yourself, scanned from a roll of something like Kodak Gold or Portra (now [also sold as Ektacolor Pro](/blog/ektacolor-pro-vs-portra)). Pick one with skin, sky and greenery in it, so the filter has a full range of colours to learn from. If Portra is the look you're after, we cover [what it really looks like](/blog/portra-look-on-phone) first.

Samsung describes My Filters in terms of colour and style, so treat it as the colour half of a film look. For texture, go to step 3.

## Step 3: Add grain and glow

Film's texture is what a colour filter can't give you. On a Galaxy, the free route is **Snapseed**. Google's Play Store listing promises no subscriptions, purchases, ads or watermarks, and includes Grain, Halation and Bloom tools.

You have two ways to use it:

- **Edit after.** Shoot with your My Filter, then add grain and a touch of halation in Snapseed. Keep them subtle.
- **Shoot through it.** Snapseed Camera lets you shoot through 11 film styles, from KP1 (Kodak Portra 400) to AS1 (Agfa Scala 200), and change the look later. Here's our full guide to [Snapseed Camera on Android](/blog/snapseed-camera-android).

When you add texture, get the details right. Grain should [change with the tones, not sit on top like an overlay](/blog/film-grain-vs-digital-noise). Halation should be a red-orange halo [around the brightest lights only](/blog/what-is-halation). And if you want a softer glow everywhere, that's bloom, the look [a Black Pro-Mist filter](/blog/black-pro-mist-filter-explained) creates.

## Or skip the steps: use a film camera app

If you'd rather not build a look from parts, a dedicated film camera app does it in one go. On Android, options include ELMAR, launched in May 2026, and FilmRoll, a new arrival on Google Play this autumn with rolls of film, 35 stocks and a darkroom. Our [film camera apps for Android roundup](/blog/film-camera-apps-for-android-2026) covers what's out there and what to look for.

## How to tell if it's working

Shoot the same scene with your Galaxy's standard camera and with your new setup, then check:

1. **Highlights** should roll off gently instead of clipping to white.
2. **Shadows** should be allowed to go dark.
3. **Skin, greens and skies** should shift differently, not all take on the same tint.
4. **Grain** should be visible but not identical everywhere.

That's a short version of [the six tells of a real film look](/blog/what-makes-photos-look-like-film).
